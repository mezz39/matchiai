# Matchlai: Product Definition

## 1. Who is the primary user?

The job seeker. This could be:

- A student or graduate who is seeking a specific career.
- An engineer who was laid off and is searching for opportunities.

In general, the user is a person who wants to improve and increase their chances of finding a job.

## 2. What problem is the product solving?

*Suggested:* the person cannot specify well where they are and what they should do to match and improve on the job requirements they are applying for.

## 3. What is the most important user journey?

**Seeker journey**

1. CV gaps analysis ("formats, results showing")
2. Knowing the future career
3. What to add or improve
4. Evaluate CV against one or many job offers **[SCORE]**
5. Testing seeker knowledge of the CV in Q/A form
6. Tracking progress to the goal
7. Learning recommendations to advance to a target

See [02-main-journey.md](02-main-journey.md) for the inputs and outputs of each step.

## 4. Where is the biggest friction?

The biggest friction is the **job match score** (step 4). It is the moment the user decides whether the product helped, and if the score is not trusted, users leave before they reach later steps.

**Tracking progress** (step 6) was the first answer. It has the most assumptions, but much of its friction is inherited from the score, because the north-star metric (advancement) is calculated from it. It remains the biggest long-term risk.

See [03-defining-friction.md](03-defining-friction.md) for the method and the scores, and [07-match-score-system.md](07-match-score-system.md) for the design of the score.

## 5. What metric matters most?

1. Advancement (**north star**)
2. Tracking completion
3. Feedback quality
4. Workflow feedback

See [04-metrics.md](04-metrics.md) for the definitions and formulas.
